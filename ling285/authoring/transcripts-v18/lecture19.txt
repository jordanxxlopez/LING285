9|**Guessing a hidden word is how an LLM learns.** The model reads “The chef cracks the” and has to say what follows. >> Every earlier word is a clue, so the hidden word depends on all of them. These relationships are **interdependencies**. || **The guess is a set of bets.** The model gives a probability to each possible word and puts the most on “egg.” >> “Nut” and “code” get less because the earlier words make them less likely. || **Each revealed answer nudges the weights.** The change is small, and it makes the right word a little more likely next time. >> The signal can hold sounds and images as well as words, and the learning works the same way.
10|**Only text arrives ready for the model.** Its words are separate pieces in a row, and each piece can become a vector. >> “Now crack the egg” is four pieces. || **Speech and images arrive without any pieces.** Speech is one continuous change in air pressure, and an image is a grid of light. >> Neither one has words in it. || **The model reads only rows of vectors.** Speech and images therefore have to be converted first. >> The conversion gives each of them the same form as text.
11|**Cutting speech into frames gives it pieces.** A frame is a slice of sound about 20 milliseconds long. >> The cuts fall at regular times. || **The speech encoder changes each slice into a vector.** It is a network trained on a large amount of audio. >> One slice goes in and one vector comes out. || **A second of speech is 50 vectors in time order.** That row can be read like a sentence. >> The earliest sound is first in the row.
12|**Cutting an image into patches gives it pieces.** A patch is a small square of the image. >> The egg image is cut into 16 patches. || **Reading order makes the patches a sequence.** They are taken left to right along the top and then along each line below. >> A sequence has a first piece and a last piece, as a sentence does. || **The vision encoder gives one vector per patch.** Real systems use patches of 14 to 16 pixels and get hundreds of vectors from one image. >> The example uses large patches only to stay simple.
13|**One row can hold all three senses.** Image patches, speech frames and words stand in a single sequence with the last word hidden. >> The words are “Now,” “crack” and “the.” || **Clues cross from one sense to another.** A patch containing an egg and the sound of cracking both point to the hidden word. >> These links are **cross-modal dependencies**. || **The model plays the same game.** It guesses the hidden piece from everything else in the row. >> The earlier words still help, as they do in a row of text.
14|**One event can be seen, heard and read.** “Three cute puppies” is an image, a spoken phrase and three written words. >> The image is cut into 18 patches. || **The spectrogram is the spoken phrase laid out in time.** Higher means higher frequency, and darker means more energy. >> Each thin column is a frame, and the words follow one another from left to right. || **Training places the three views in one row.** One piece of the row is hidden for the model to guess. >> The same example is used for a hidden word, a hidden frame and a hidden patch.
15|**Words alone leave the model unsure.** After “three cute,” the bets on “kittens,” “puppies” and “babies” are close together. >> Many endings fit those two words. || **The image and the sound settle the guess.** The image contains puppies and the sound contains the word, so the bet piles up on “puppies.” >> These are clues from other senses. || **A good bet gives a small nudge.** The model bet 0.85 on the true word, so the error is small. >> The weights move only a little.
16|**The model can also guess the next frame of sound.** It works through the sound from left to right, one frame at a time. >> This is like teaching the model to speak a sentence it was given. || **The sound itself is the strongest clue.** The voice and the formants change smoothly, so recent frames say a lot about the next one. >> The words give the next sound, the vowel in “pup,” and the image backs up the words. || **The error is the gap between guess and truth.** A frame is a column of energy values, one per frequency band. >> The guess is close but not exact, and the gap nudges the weights.
17|**The model can also guess the next patch of an image.** It works through the patches in reading order. >> This is like teaching the model to draw what it was told. || **The clues give the content of the patch.** Two puppies are already drawn, “three” calls for one more, and “puppies” calls for dog ears. >> The sound carries the same words as a backup. || **No clue gave the colour.** The guess takes the average colour of the first two puppies, but the third puppy is dark. >> The colour is the biggest error, and it nudges the weights.
18|**Each sense gives a clue for each kind of hidden piece.** That makes nine clues for a hidden word, a hidden frame and a hidden patch. >> For a hidden word they are a plural noun, the word just spoken, and puppies in the image. || **A clue from the same sense says how the signal continues.** Grammar continues a sentence, smooth formants continue a sound, and fur and background continue an image. >> These three are the same-sense clues. || **Clues from the other senses say what the thing is.** They say puppies, three of them. >> Each sense fills gaps the others leave.
19|**Speech output uses codes.** Sound is compressed into discrete codes that work like new words in the vocabulary. >> The model predicts the next code as it predicts the next word. || **A decoder makes the codes audible.** It changes the row of codes back into a sound wave. >> The model itself only predicts the codes. || **Three things are new.** They are a shared currency of vectors in one row, data where the senses appear together, and a schedule that protects what was already learned. >> What stays the same is finding the dependencies in the signal.
