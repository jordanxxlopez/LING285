9|**An LLM learns from a hidden word.** In “The chef cracks the,” each earlier word gives a clue about the word that comes next. >> These clue relationships between parts of a sequence are **interdependencies**. || **The model spreads its bets over the possible words.** “Egg” gets the largest bet, “nut” a smaller one and “code” the smallest. >> A bet is the probability the model gives to one word. || **Learning nudges the weights so the guesses improve.** After the true word is revealed, the right word gets a bigger bet next time. >> The same learning applies when the signal holds words, sounds and images.
10|**Text is already a row of pieces.** “Now crack the egg” separates into four pieces, and each piece can become a vector. >> This makes text the easy case for an LLM. || **Speech and images arrive in other forms.** Speech is air pressure changing in time, and an image is a grid of light. >> Neither one contains words. || **Both have to be converted into rows of vectors.** A row of vectors is all the LLM can read. >> The conversion comes before any learning from speech or images.
11|**Speech is sliced into short frames.** Each frame is a slice of sound about 20 milliseconds long. >> The slices are cut by time and do not follow the words. || **A speech encoder gives one vector per slice.** The encoder is a neural network trained on a large amount of audio. >> Different sounds give vectors with different numbers. || **One second of speech becomes 50 vectors in time order.** The speech is now a row, just like a sentence. >> Fifty slices of about 20 milliseconds fill one second.
12|**An image is cut into patches.** A patch is one small square, and the egg image is cut into 16 of them. >> The grid is 4 across and 4 down. || **The patches are laid out in reading order.** The image becomes a sequence, like a sentence. >> Reading order means left to right along the top and then along each line below. || **A vision encoder gives one vector per patch.** Real systems use patches of 14 to 16 pixels. >> One image therefore gives hundreds of vectors in a real system.
13|**Image patches, speech frames and words share one row.** The last word after “Now crack the” is hidden. >> The patches come first, the frames second and the words last. || **The earlier words still give clues, and the other senses add more.** A patch containing an egg and the sound of cracking both help. >> A clue that links two different senses is a **cross-modal dependency**. || **The game is the same as before.** The model guesses the hidden piece from the pieces it can read. >> Only the source of some clues has changed.
14|**“Three cute puppies” is given in three forms.** They are an image, the spoken phrase and the written words. >> The image is cut into 18 patches, 3 rows of 6. || **The spoken phrase is a spectrogram.** Time runs left to right, higher means higher frequency, and darker means more energy. >> Each thin column is one frame, and each word has its own stretch of time. || **The three forms are three views of one event.** Training puts all of them in one row. >> The model then guesses a hidden piece of that row.
15|**The hidden piece is the word after “three cute.”** The row holds the image, the sound and the words. >> With the words alone, the bets are spread over “kittens,” “puppies” and “babies.” || **The image and the sound move the bet onto “puppies.”** The image contains puppies, and the sound contains the spoken word. >> The other two words are left with very small bets. || **The model bet 0.85 on the true word, so the nudge is small.** A large bet on the truth is a small error. >> A small error moves the weights only a little.
16|**The hidden piece is a frame of sound.** The row holds the image, the words and the sound heard so far. >> The sound is predicted frame by frame from left to right. || **Three clues point to the next frame.** The earlier frames help because the voice and the formants change smoothly. >> The words say the next sound is the vowel in “pup,” and the image backs up the words. || **The error nudges the weights.** A frame is a column of numbers for the energy in each frequency band. >> The error is how far the guessed numbers are from the true ones.
17|**The hidden piece is a patch of the image.** The row holds the words, the sound and the patches drawn so far. >> The patches are predicted in reading order. || **The clues give a puppy ear but not its colour.** The earlier patches give background and two puppies, and “three” says one more puppy must appear. >> “Puppies” says to expect dog ears, and the sound backs up the words. || **The biggest error is the colour.** The guess uses the average colour of the first two puppies, and the true puppy is dark. >> No clue said the third puppy would be dark, so that error nudges the weights.
18|**Every piece can be the hidden one.** A hidden word, frame or patch each receives a clue from the words, the sound and the image. >> For a hidden word, the three clues are a plural noun, the word just spoken, and puppies in the image. || **The same sense says how the signal continues.** Grammar, smooth formants, and fur and background all carry on from what came before. >> These are the clues for a hidden word, frame and patch from their own sense. || **The other senses say what the thing is.** Here they say puppies, three of them. >> Each sense fills gaps the others leave, which is why training on all three together helps.
19|**Sound is compressed into discrete codes so the model can speak.** Each code works like a new word in the vocabulary. >> The model predicts the next code like the next word. || **A decoder changes the codes back into sound.** The output is a sound wave that can be heard. >> Speaking is therefore the same guessing game followed by decoding. || **Three things are new, and the learning rule is unchanged.** The new things are a shared currency of vectors in one row, data where the senses appear together, and a schedule that protects what was already learned. >> The model still finds the dependencies in the signal.
